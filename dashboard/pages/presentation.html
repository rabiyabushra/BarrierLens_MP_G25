Section 02 · Research Gap & Contribution

The Gap: From Retrospective Counts to Predictive Decision Intelligence

While foundational epidemiology identified aggregate barrier rates, public health administrators lacked individual-level prediction, sub-population clustering, downstream outcome links, and conversational access.

The Base Paper (Pradhan & De, 2025)

Descriptive & Bivariate Benchmark

  • Quantified macroscopic barrier prevalence at state and national levels.
  • Limited to retrospective survey counts without individual risk scoring.
  • No machine learning models to infer barrier risk for newly enrolled women.
  • No segmentation into compound vulnerability archetypes.
  • Static tables without actionable policy routing or multilingual interaction.
BarrierLens Innovation (Our Contribution)

Two-Stage Predictive & Multilingual Intelligence

  • Stage 1 ML: Trains frozen ensemble models (XGBoost, RF) predicting 3 barrier probabilities per individual.
  • Archetype Discovery: Unsupervised K-Means clustering identifying distinct vulnerability profiles.
  • Stage 2 Downstream Links: Quantifies predictive association with unmet family planning and antenatal care gaps.
  • Audited Conversational Assistant: Multi-turn multilingual RAG in English, Hindi, and Kannada citing allowlisted schemes.
Scope boundary: BarrierLens specifically addresses women's structural access to healthcare services across India, using individual-recode survey data.

🎙️ Presenter Script · Section 04 (1:45 – 2:20)
Here is the pipeline architecture. We take raw survey records, clean and encode them, and train Stage 1 models. But notice step 3 to 4: why out-of-fold probabilities? If we trained Stage 2 or clustering on the same in-sample predictions that Stage 1 saw during training, the model would overfit dramatically. Out-of-fold cross-validation gives us honest, leak-free probability estimates that feed directly into K-Means clustering and our Stage 2 downstream impact models.

Section 09 · Core Differentiators

What Makes BarrierLens Novel: Four Architectural Pillars

BarrierLens distinguishes itself from conventional dashboard analytics and generative chatbots through four rigorous design pillars.

🔗

1. Connects Data Directly to Action

Conventional dashboards present retrospective problem counts. BarrierLens pairs every identified barrier with operational, funded government schemes (e.g. NHM, JSSK, PMSMA) stored in a verified registry.

🛡️

2. Dual Evidence Types Kept Strictly Separate

Internal empirical survey metrics (Rail A observed / Rail B predicted) and external policy solutions are maintained in separate data schemas and rendered in distinct visual styles to avoid confusion.

🔒

3. Language Model Never Generates Statistics

All numerical statistics are pre-computed and dynamically injected into prompts. Post-processing safety validators cross-verify that every number matches the evidence payload and replace causal claims with associative phrasing.

🌐

4. Trilingual Natural Language Interface

Full conversational query capability in English, Hindi, and Kannada, allowing grassroots health officers and field workers to ask questions in everyday vernacular without needing SQL or Python skills.

🎙️ Presenter Script · Section 09 (4:45 – 5:15)
To summarize our novelty: first, we connect data to immediate policy action. Second, we keep empirical data strictly separate from external scheme recommendations. Third, the language model never invents statistics—numbers are injected from verified JSON files, and causal claims are sanitized. Fourth, it works trilingually in English, Hindi, and Kannada.

Section 12 · Technical Backup & Methodological Appendix

Technical Appendix & Scope Notes

Detailed technical specifications, cross-validation parameters, cluster selection criteria, and epidemiological scope limitations.

1. Stage 1 Model Architecture & Hyperparameters ▼

Stage 1 evaluated Logistic Regression (L2 penalty, C=1.0), Decision Trees (max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=50), Random Forest (200 estimators, max_features='sqrt'), and XGBoost (learning_rate=0.08, max_depth=6, n_estimators=250, colsample_bytree=0.8). 5-fold stratified cross-validation was enforced on 724,115 records.

2. Rural vs Urban Granular Disparity Breakdown ▼

Logistic barriers exhibit the steepest urban-rural divide: 35.8% in rural vs 20.6% in urban areas (a 15.2% point gap). Facility deficits affect 48.9% rural vs 39.0% urban women. Household autonomy barriers affect 28.9% rural vs 22.9% urban women.

3. K-Means Optimal Cluster Selection (k=2 Silhouette vs Elbow) ▼

Cluster k was selected through systematic evaluation of k ∈ [2, 6]. k=2 achieved the global maximum silhouette score of 0.482, cleanly separating respondents into baseline risk versus high compound vulnerability. k=3 and k=4 yielded fragmented sub-clusters with diminished silhouette separation (<0.32).

4. Stage 2 Downstream Impact Architecture ▼

Stage 2 models evaluate whether out-of-fold predicted barrier probabilities predict downstream reproductive outcomes over and above basic sociodemographic controls. Logistic regression and Random Forest were benchmarked on Unmet Need for Family Planning (v626a) and <4 ANC Visits (m14_1).

5. Conversational Safety & Hallucination Mitigation ▼

The RAG assistant implements a dual-stage safety filter: (1) Prompt-side injection of pre-computed verified statistics from JSON files, and (2) Post-generation validation via backend/services/safety_validator.py. The validator strips causal language, sanitizes URLs against allowlisted health domains, verifies that numerical claims match the evidence payload, and enforces medical disclaimer attachment.

6. Scope Notes, Cross-Sectional Nature & Survey Extraction Limitations ▼

Epidemiological Scope Notes: All findings are derived from NFHS-5 (2019–21) cross-sectional survey data. Statistical relationships represent predictive associations, not causal proofs. Barrier encounters are self-reported by respondents. Recommended public schemes are sourced from published operational guidelines and have not been individually evaluated for local execution effectiveness. Two questionnaire items (v467f and v467i) were not uniformly available in the national extract and were substituted with corresponding verified composite metrics.
